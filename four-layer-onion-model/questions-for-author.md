# Questions for the author: *Universal Logic and the Four-Layer Onion*

Mark,

Thank you for sharing the letter this paper is built from. We read the draft the way an
alignment researcher or a Hegel scholar reviewing it would. The argument reads
clearly, and the four layers are a useful way to organize the ideas. To make
the strongest possible case, we need your answers to some questions that only
you can settle.

**How to answer.** Short answers are fine. "I don't know yet" is a useful
answer too, because it tells us what to present as open. Reply under each
question or by email using the numbers. Links to documents, transcripts, or
data are welcome.

The questions come in three parts:

- **Part 1** has five questions. The case rests on them, so please answer these
  first.
- **Part 2** contains short questions about facts and definitions; each
  needs a line or two.
- **Part 3** is optional and deepens the argument if you have time.

Part 4 lists the changes we plan to make ourselves. It needs no answer unless
you object to something.

---

## Part 1. The five questions the case rests on

**1. The ULM, or any explicit ontology?** Is the claim that AI needs the
Universal Logic Map specifically? Or is it that any explicit, ordered,
machine-checkable ontology helps, and the ULM is the best candidate? The second
framing is much easier to defend: the four layers then stand as a general
method even for readers who reject Hegel, and the ULM becomes the ontology
under test. Would you accept that framing?

**2. What is WoW?** The letter and the paper both refer to the game WoW, but
neither explains the game. Please tell us what the name stands for, how a round
works, how a player is corrected, and why the protocol uses 20 to 650 rounds.
If transcripts of games exist, with people or with AI models, please share
the transcripts.

**3. How does an everyday concept get a place on the map?** The Layer 2 gate
compares a response with the ULM, but most responses never mention Being or
Logic. For example, how would "switch to solar and wind," "profit," or "my
child" each be assigned a position among the 654? If no procedure exists yet,
a few worked examples would help.

**4. Where do the priorities come from?** The paper says the ordering puts
human welfare above profit and environmental repair above profit. Does that
follow from the logical order, and if so, through which steps? Or is it your
reading of what the order implies? Philosophers will ask how an ordering of
categories produces an ordering of values, so this needs a clear answer.

**5. What exists today?** Is there a machine-readable version of the ULM (its
positions, links, and distances)? Who built it, and could it be published? Is
there data of any kind, even informal, such as WoW sessions, tribe choices from
people or models, ELM meeting ratings, or a prototype parser? A small amount of
real data would strengthen the paper more than any other change.

## Part 2. Short facts and definitions

**6.** What is the **Vow of Unabandonment**? One or two sentences are enough.

**7.** What does **ELM** stand for, and have ELM meetings been run? If so, with
about how many people?

**8.** The **metamodes of consciousness** are art, religion, philosophy,
science, and pure Universal Logic. The first three are Hegel's Absolute Spirit.
Why do you add science and Universal Logic?

**9.** Where does the count of **about 654 positions** come from? Is there a
list that maps each position to a passage in Hegel?

**10.** **Depths on the map.** Figure 2 places Logic at depth 0, Nature at 3,
and Spirit at 6, while the scoring formula uses depths 1 to 9. Which is right,
and what is the maximum depth?

**11.** **The climate example.** Are the participants real people or an
illustration? In the letter, Participant 2 is Amanda; should she stay
anonymous? The letter calls her ordering both "tribe 3" and "Nature, Spirit,
Logic," which is Tribe 4 in the table. Which is correct?

**12.** **The 85% figure.** Does the estimate that about 85% of humanity puts
Spirit first come from Pew's finding that 84% of people have a religious
affiliation? If so, we will cite Pew and note that affiliation is not the same
as ranking Spirit first.

**13.** **The numbers in the paper.** What reasoning lies behind the 0 to 10
ratings in Table 1 (for example, 9.5 versus 9.9)? And what is the basis for the
savings of $10M to $40M per training run and 10 to 20% less compute? If they
are rough judgments, we will present them as rankings and projections.

**14.** **TempleOS.** What does TempleOS contribute to Layer 4? Readers in ML
will expect the Gödel Machine and its recent successors here, which we plan to
add. Should TempleOS stay?

## Part 3. Optional: questions that deepen the argument

**15. Known failures.** Choose two or three problems that alignment
researchers already study: flattering users, deception, gaming a reward, or
seeking power. For each problem, which misordering of categories causes it, and what
would a ULM-ordered model do differently from a model that follows an ordinary
list of written principles? A side-by-side example would be the most
persuasive addition to the paper.

**16. Value conflicts.** Does the ordering settle conflicts that written
principles leave open, such as honesty against kindness, or a user's freedom
against possible harm? One worked case would be enough.

**17. Layers 3B and 4 in practice.** What would "anchoring the weights" involve
in training: an extra term in the loss, or a change to specific internal
representations? For Layer 4, what would you propose beyond applying the audit
to the model's own code changes?

**18. Hegel's three syllogisms.** The *Encyclopaedia* ends with three
syllogisms, each with a different one of Logic, Nature, and Spirit in the
middle, and Hegel treats all three as valid. The Tribe test marks two of those
orderings as fragmented. Why is Tribe 5 the privileged one?

**19. The top-level claim.** What makes you expect that a choice among three
words predicts coherence across all 654 positions? Has anyone's tribe choice
been compared with other measures of how that person thinks?

**20. Your reading of Hegel.** Which interpreters do you build on or argue
against, such as Houlgate, Pippin, or Brandom? Brandom's reading has already
been connected to language models, so it may be a useful bridge. How does the
Proof of Truth differ from Hegel's own beginning with pure being?

**21. Why Hegel?** What is your reply to "why Hegel's system rather than
Kant's categories, Whitehead, or an engineered ontology such as BFO"? The paper
says that reordering Logic, Nature, and Spirit creates contradictions; one
concrete example would carry that section.

**22. Revision and lock-in.** Many safety researchers fear that building one
fixed worldview into all AI systems would be hard to undo. Who would revise the
ULM, how, and on what evidence?

**23. Disagreement.** How should people who reject the ordering be
handled under the framework? In the climate example, the outcome is that the others come to accept
Tribe 5. Is the aim persuasion or mediation?

**24. Objections you have heard.** What are the strongest objections people
have raised, including Amanda's, and how did you answer them? Your answers
would become an "Objections and Replies" section.

**25. Venue and priorities.** Where would you like this to appear: an AI safety
workshop, a philosophy of AI journal, or a public preprint? Which claims are
central for you, and which could be softened or cut, such as the human-group
predictions, the economic projections, or the psilocybin conjecture?

## Part 4. Changes we plan to make (no answer needed)

Tell us if you object to any of these.

**Standard terms.** We will use the names that ML readers already know, and
mention our own names where helpful:

| Current wording | Planned wording | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Semantic Logic Mapping (SLM) | a new name, such as "response graph" | In ML, SLM means "small language model" |
| sandbagging | alignment faking | Sandbagging means underperforming on purpose in tests; claiming compliance while doing otherwise is called alignment faking |
| harness | scaffold | Standard term for code that runs a model from outside |
| unignorability | tamper resistance | Standard term |
| efficiency | alignment tax | Standard term for the cost of making a model safe |
| separation of powers | trusted monitoring | Standard term in "AI control" research |
| choosing the best of many candidate responses | best-of-N sampling | Standard term |
| regenerate with a constraint signal | critique and revision | Standard term |
| unprimed | zero-shot | Standard term |
| "metamap" approach | another name | MetaMap is an existing tool that links medical text to an ontology |
| Layers 1 to 4 | possibly "levels" | ML readers may confuse these with the layers of a neural network |

**Fixes to the scoring method.** We will handle responses that mention no
mapped concept (the current formula divides by zero) and words like "cat" that
are not ULM positions. We will also explain how to set the threshold, since one
wrong link in twelve currently fails a response.

**Related work.** We will connect each layer to recent research, in many cases
as support for your argument:

- **The audit gate (Layer 2):** Anthropic's Constitutional Classifiers (2025),
  "AI control" with trusted monitoring (Greenblatt et al., 2024), and work on
  matching one ontology to another.
- **Cleaning the training data (Layer 3A):** *Safety Pretraining* (Maini et
  al., 2025) rewrites unsafe text and tags it during pre-training, much like
  your two variants. *Deep Ignorance* (O'Brien et al., 2025) found that
  filtering training data resists later tampering better than safety training
  added afterwards, which supports the claim that inner layers are harder to
  bypass.
- **The weights (Layer 3B):** Park et al. (2025) found that language models
  already arrange hierarchical concepts in a regular geometry. We will also
  cite research on concept bottleneck models and on how models represent
  concepts internally.
- **Self-modification (Layer 4):** the Darwin Gödel Machine (Zhang et al.,
  2025) and earlier work on keeping goals stable when a system rewrites itself.
- **Prompts are not enough (Layer 1):** *Alignment faking in large language
  models* (Greenblatt et al., 2024) and related findings that models do not
  always report their reasoning faithfully.
- **The Tribe test and the climate example:** Schwartz's theory of basic human
  values, which is itself a ranking of values, and the *Habermas Machine*
  (Tessler et al., 2024), an AI mediator that helped groups find common ground,
  including on climate change.
- **Philosophy:** the principle that "determination is negation," which Hegel
  quotes from Spinoza and which matches your "law of determining," and
  Houlgate's work on Hegel's presuppositionless beginning.

**Name clash.** "Universal logic" is also the name of a field in mathematical
logic (Béziau's *Logica Universalis*). We will mention this once so that
readers do not confuse the two.

Thank you. Answers to Part 1 alone would let us strengthen the paper
substantially.
